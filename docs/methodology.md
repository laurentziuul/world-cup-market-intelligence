# Methodology

## Goal

The system studies how prediction markets absorb information during a scheduled global event.

It does not attempt to become a football model. It observes market behavior.

## Signal categories

### Structural

A signal is structural when it changes the base conditions of the market.

Examples:

- confirmed injury to a key player
- qualification format implication
- major liquidity migration
- market-wide repricing across related outcomes
- schedule/travel/rest asymmetry that affects many matches

### Tactical

A signal is tactical when it matters for a short window.

Examples:

- lineup speculation
- pre-match odds drift
- market overreaction after one result
- public narrative spike

### Speculative

A signal is speculative when it is mostly narrative or weakly confirmed.

Examples:

- social media rumor
- fanbase hype
- thin market move
- low-liquidity price jump

## Red-team questions

Before publishing a signal, answer:

1. Is there enough liquidity behind the move?
2. Is the market reacting to confirmed information or rumor?
3. Is the price move isolated or visible across related markets?
4. Could this be pure public fan bias?
5. What would make this interpretation wrong?
6. Is this useful as research even if it is not tradable?

## Confidence levels

- High: confirmed catalyst + liquid market + cross-market confirmation
- Medium: plausible catalyst + some liquidity + partial confirmation
- Low: thin market + weak catalyst + narrative-heavy movement
